Taming a Star

In the Fusion Plasma Auxiliaries Characterization Lab, I worked with data from KSTAR, a Korean reactor that briefly recreates the conditions inside a star, to help with reactor wall stability.

Overview

Fusion is the power source of stars, and the hardest part is not starting the reaction but containing it. The plasma-facing wall has to survive heat and particle loads beyond almost anything else humans build. My work was on the data side of keeping that wall stable.

I joined the FPAC lab as a freshman and spent a semester on a project analyzing spectroscopy and video data from KSTAR experiment campaigns, building the processing pipeline that turned raw signals into something the researchers could actually interpret.

The research

The question

When the plasma interacts with the reactor wall, the evidence shows up in the light it emits. Spotting those events cleanly in noisy spectroscopy data is the first step to understanding what the wall is going through.

The data

Time-series spectroscopy and camera video from KSTAR shots. I wrote Python workflows for the signal processing, including adaptive Gaussian smoothing that noticeably improved peak detection over the fixed-width filtering we started with.

The reliability angle

This was reliability engineering before I had the words for it: predicting when a critical component survives and when it does not.
