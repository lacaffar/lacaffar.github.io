Overview

A flight computer and ground station I am building on my own bench. The board logs flight data at 100 Hz and streams live telemetry over a 915 MHz LoRa link to a Raspberry Pi on the ground, which serves a real-time dashboard I can reach from anywhere over a Tailscale mesh.

The honest reason I started it: live telemetry off something moving that fast looked just hard enough to be interesting. Fusion research and wind reliability both taught me the same lesson from the data side, which is that you only understand a failure if you instrumented for it beforehand. This is me building the instrument.

The stack

Flight computer

A Teensy 4.1 reading a 9-DOF IMU, a barometer for altitude, and GPS for position, writing a 100 Hz log to onboard SD so the full record survives even if the radio link does not. Firmware in embedded C++.

The link

915 MHz LoRa for the live downlink. Long range and low bandwidth, which forces real decisions about what is worth sending in flight versus what can wait for the SD card after recovery.

Ground station

A Raspberry Pi receives the downlink and serves a live dashboard. It sits on a Tailscale mesh VPN, so the dashboard is reachable remotely without exposing anything to the open internet.

Where it stands

Flight testing the full stack on a quadcopter first, because a quadcopter can be flown repeatedly, hovered at a fixed altitude for sensor checks, and landed without a recovery walk. Once the logging and the link hold up there, it flies on a high-power rocket.
