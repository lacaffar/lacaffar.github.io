# lacaffar.github.io

Personal site for Luke Caffarey. Static HTML and CSS, no build step and no JavaScript.

The site is deliberately presented as in progress: project pages are marked as drafts,
each one carries a "still to write" list, and projects without a page appear on the index
as stubs rather than being padded out with detail that is not true.

- `index.html` : both panels, the portfolio index and the narrative timeline
- `projects/*.html` : one page per project
- `assets/css/site.css` : the whole design system
- `assets/covers/` : project cover art

The two portraits that open the narrative are cut by `.claude/make_parent_photos.py`
from `assets/photos/parent-{one,two}.jpg`. They sit on black, not on paper, so unlike
the project covers they get no warm shift: only a little contrast, because a 120px face
on black loses its modelling, and a little off the saturation so the two backgrounds sit
together.

`projects/windpack.html` is the one fully written page: season one narrative, the
subsystem build, what broke, and the year two plan. Its photos are cut by
`.claude/make_windpack_photos.py` from 1600px sources staged in `assets/photos/`.
The team's own site is <https://windpack.club>.

The index is two panels driven by one hidden checkbox and no script: `#portfolio` is the
project list, `#narrative` is the timeline (year and title only, no prose).

The map in each bottom corner is a **photograph of that panel**, not a drawing of it, and
not a link: `assets/minimap/{portfolio,narrative}.png`. Retake both after any visible
change with

```
python .claude/make_minimaps.py
```

which serves the repo, drives headless Chrome over each panel with the dock hidden and the
opening animation held at its end, and writes the two PNGs. If the page gets much longer
or shorter, nudge the `.mm-view` height and `--travel` in `site.css`: they are the share of
the map one screen covers, and the distance it has left to run.

The narrative opens with the overture: two portraits meet, a dot is left behind, and it
bounces three times before the pen carries it along a drawn line into the timeline. That
line is generated by `.claude/make_overture_path.py`, which prints the `d` string. It has
to be pasted in **two** places that must stay identical, or the dot will drift off the
line: the `.ov-path` `d` in `index.html` and the `offset-path` in `site.css`.

The line is **one parametric curve in five stages**, walked once from `t=0` to `t=5`:
straight up `L1`, a quarter turn counter clockwise at `R1`, a full turn clockwise whose
radius opens from `R2` to `R3`, a three quarter turn clockwise at `R4`, and a straight
`L2` down onto the rail. Each stage leaves on the heading the next one arrives with, so
it is a single drawn line rather than five pieces laid end to end.

The spiral's radius opens as `r(u) = R2 + (R3 - R2) u^2`, and the square is not
decoration. It holds the radius nearly flat through the first half turn, which is the
half that goes over the top. A spiral that opened linearly would have spent the headroom
before it was a quarter of the way round.

Two of the sliders are not free. In the stage's own pixels, origin at the dot and y
counted up the page, the line finishes at `x = -R1 + R4` and
`y = L1 + R1 + R2 - R3 + R4 - L2`, so:

- **`R4 = R1` is what puts the last straight under the dot.** Break that tie and the line
  ends off to one side of the rail, and the dot has to be dragged back to meet it.
- **`L2` is not chosen, it is what is left** between the curl and the rail once the four
  stages above it have been placed. The script solves it.
- **Headroom decides everything else.** The top of the spiral sits about
  `L1 + R1 + 1.63 R2 + 0.29 R3` above the dot and there are only 150px to spend. Spent on
  `R3` they buy a long fall and a short dull straight at the end; spent on `R1` and `R2`
  they buy a bigger opening hook and a bigger closing curl, at about five pixels of that
  straight tail for every pixel of radius.
- **The joins are tangent continuous, not curvature continuous.** Curvature flips sign
  where the hook hands over to the spiral, and steps from 1/296 to 1/15 going into the
  curl. That is what the formulation asks for, and at these radii it reads as a hook and
  a curl rather than as a fault. It is worth knowing it is there, though: the line this
  replaced was a curvature program that ramped every join rather than stepping it, which
  is the other way to build this and the way to go back to if a join ever looks bent.
- **Nothing drawn by hand sits on its own ideal.** The curve above is only where the
  line is aiming. It is then sampled, every sample is pushed sideways by a little noise,
  and a centripetal Catmull Rom runs through what is left. Take the wobble out and the
  line goes back to looking printed.
- **The noise is in arc length, not sample index.** That is what keeps the wobble the
  same size in pixels whether it lands on the curl, where the samples are 7px apart, or
  on the straight, where they are 26px. Four sines with no common period, so it never
  repeats; the amplitude comes down where the line is tight, because 1.4px on a 15px
  curl is a lump rather than a wobble; and it tapers to nothing at both ends, so the dot
  still leaves the bounce straight up and still meets the rail straight down.
- **Sampling has to follow curvature.** 26px between fit points is fine down the long
  fall and far too coarse round the curl, where it cuts the corner off the very curve
  this was built to keep smooth. The spacing is jittered on top of that, because a hand
  does not put its marks down on a ruler either.
