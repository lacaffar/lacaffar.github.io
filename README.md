# lacaffar.github.io

Personal site for Luke Caffarey. Static HTML and CSS, no build step and no JavaScript.

The site is deliberately presented as in progress: project pages are marked as drafts,
each one carries a "still to write" list, and projects without a page appear on the index
as stubs rather than being padded out with detail that is not true.

- `index.html` : both panels, the portfolio index and the narrative timeline
- `projects/*.html` : one page per project
- `assets/css/site.css` : the whole design system
- `assets/covers/` : project cover art

The two portraits that open the narrative are cut by `.claude/make_parent_photos.py`
from `assets/photos/parent-{one,two}.jpg`. They sit on black, not on paper, so unlike
the project covers they get no warm shift: only a little contrast, because a 120px face
on black loses its modelling, and a little off the saturation so the two backgrounds sit
together.

`projects/windpack.html` is the one fully written page: season one narrative, the
subsystem build, what broke, and the year two plan. Its photos are cut by
`.claude/make_windpack_photos.py` from 1600px sources staged in `assets/photos/`.
The team's own site is <https://windpack.club>.

The index is two panels driven by one hidden checkbox and no script: `#portfolio` is the
project list, `#narrative` is the timeline (year and title only, no prose).

The map in each bottom corner is a **photograph of that panel**, not a drawing of it, and
not a link: `assets/minimap/{portfolio,narrative}.png`. Retake both after any visible
change with

```
python .claude/make_minimaps.py
```

which serves the repo, drives headless Chrome over each panel with the dock hidden and the
opening animation held at its end, and writes the two PNGs. If the page gets much longer
or shorter, nudge the `.mm-view` height and `--travel` in `site.css`: they are the share of
the map one screen covers, and the distance it has left to run.

The narrative opens with the overture: two portraits meet, a dot is left behind, and it
bounces three times before the pen carries it along a drawn line into the timeline. That
line is generated by `.claude/make_overture_path.py`, which prints the `d` string. It has
to be pasted in **two** places that must stay identical, or the dot will drift off the
line: the `.ov-path` `d` in `index.html` and the `offset-path` in `site.css`.

The line is a **capital L**, and it is not drawn by hand. It is the centreline of a real
copperplate capital, traced off `.claude/capital_l_reference.jpg` and baked into
`.claude/capital_l_trace.py` by

```
python .claude/trace_capital_l.py > .claude/capital_l_trace.py
```

which thins the filled glyph to a one pixel skeleton and walks it end to end. The walk is
the only hard part. The letter crosses itself twice, so the skeleton is a graph and not a
thread, and a walk that just follows pixels turns off at the first crossing. Two things it
took a while to get right:

- **Neighbour count does not find the crossings.** An eight connected skeleton gives a
  plain diagonal run three neighbours, so most of the letter looks like a junction. The
  crossing number, the count of 0 to 1 transitions round the eight neighbours, does work.
- **A crossing does not thin to one pixel.** It comes out as two forks a few pixels apart
  with a stub between them, so cutting only the marked pixels leaves the four arms still
  joined through the stub. Taking everything within `NODE` of a fork is what makes the X
  fall into four arms, which can then be paired by which two carry straight on through.

So the shape of the L is the reference's exactly, and `make_overture_path.py` only decides
how big it sits, where, and the two strokes that are not part of the letter. Neither
placement number is free. The entry tip has to sit about 30px **left** of the dot, because
the reference starts on a heading of 163 degrees, very nearly straight left; a fall that
lands directly under the dot has to swing out right and come back to arrive on that
heading, and the swing is a visible kink. The scale is then bounded by what is left: the
tail tip needs height below it to straighten onto the rail, and the base loop needs a
margin on the left.

The letter is placed so its bounding box is **centred on the stage**, at (200, 310).
Vertically that lands exactly. Horizontally it cannot, because the entry tip sits almost
exactly at the letter's own half width, so centring it would put the tip under the dot and
bring the kink straight back; it ends up about 17px left, which is as close as the entry
allows. Moving the letter up shortens the fall and lengthens the run one for one, and the
run is no loss: it is the stroke that turns into the timeline rail.

Both joining strokes are a single cubic. An intermediate knot is what put a wiggle in both
of them: with the ends already carrying the letter's own tangents there is nothing left for
a middle knot to do except disagree.

There is no wobble on it. The line this replaced was a hand drawn one and was jittered to
match, but a letter is either the shape or it is not.

**The dot is caught on the way down.** It used to stop dead at the top of its third hop and
the pen would start drawing from a standstill. Now the third hop is a real one: the dot
comes off the top of it still falling, the bounce ends with it back on the line and moving,
and the pen takes it there, which is why the line starts by falling. The drop out of the
bounce and the entry stroke of the L are one movement.

That only reads if the speeds match as well as the positions. The dot arrives off the hop
at about 140px a second, so the run's easing opens at about 150 rather than the 350 a
sharper curve would give. Start it any harder and the handoff reads as a jump however well
the positions line up.

The opening hangs off one moment: the two portraits meeting, at 48% of 2.7s. The pop, the
bounce, the draw and the run all follow from it, and the draw is pinned to the **end** of
the bounce, because that is the frame the dot is caught on. The dot's fade waits for the
line to land rather than thinning out part way down the last stroke.

The seven connectors between the timeline beats are generated too, by
`.claude/make_timeline_links.py`, and they are not decoration. The first three are **sharp
turns**, because the first three beats are where the plan changed and the rail should look
like it changed with them: it runs down, corners hard onto a new bearing, and corners hard
back. After that the corners open out and the swing comes in, so by the bottom of the page
the rail is only wandering again. Each one is a polyline down a 40x120 box with its two
interior corners filleted, and the swing comes down the page as the fillet goes up, so the
softening reads as both less turn and less suddenness at once. The swing always goes toward
the side the next beat sits on. The reason each of the first three turned sits across the
rail from the beat itself, in `.tl-turn`: what was wanted on one line, what happened to it
on the next.
