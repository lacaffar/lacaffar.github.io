Overview

I joined GE Vernova's onshore wind business as a wind engineer intern in May 2026. The Windpack turbines we build at school are scaled models. Here the same physics plays out at multi-ton, grid-feeding scale, where a failure is expensive and reliability is the entire point.

Most of my summer went into the data side of keeping turbines healthy: the records engineers rely on when hardware misbehaves, and the tooling that makes those records useful.

What I worked on

Search over root cause analyses

A wind fleet generates years of inspection and failure records, and the answer to a new problem is often buried in an old one. I prototyped a semantic search tool that lets engineers surface similar past failures and how they were resolved, instead of digging through documents by keyword.

Automating safety reporting

I rebuilt a weekly safety-critical reporting workflow in Python and SQL, turning a manual, several-step process into something that runs in one pass.

What reliability means here

Margins, inspections, and validation on hardware that has to run in the field for decades. Watching how a mature engineering organization treats failure has changed how I approach my own builds.
